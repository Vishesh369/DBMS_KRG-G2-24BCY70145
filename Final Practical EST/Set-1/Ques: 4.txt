QUESTION: 4
R(A, B, C, D, E, F) with FDs: AB -> C, C -> D, D -> BE, E -> F. Identify the Candidate Keys and the highest Normal Form.

ANSWER:
To find the candidate key, we start by checking what AB can determine using the given dependencies. 
From AB we get C, then C gives D, D gives E, and E gives F. In this way, AB is able to determine 
all the attributes in the relation. Also, A alone or B alone cannot determine everything, so AB is
minimal. Hence, AB is the candidate key.

Now looking at the normal form, A and B are the prime attributes, and the rest are non prime. There 
is no partial dependency because C depends on the full key AB, so the relation is in Second Normal 
Form. But after that, we can see a chain of dependencies like C to D, D to E, and E to F. This shows
that non prime attributes depend on other non prime attributes, which is a transitive dependency. 
Because of this, the relation does not satisfy Third Normal Form.

So, the candidate key is AB and the highest normal form of the relation is Second Normal Form.

Final Answer:
Candidate Key is AB
Highest Normal Form is Second Normal Form

The relation is not in Third Normal Form because transitive dependencies exist.
